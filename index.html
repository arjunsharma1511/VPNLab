Foundation

What is a VPN?

VPN means Virtual Private Network. It creates a protected connection—often described as an encrypted tunnel—across another network such as the internet.

🔐

Protect data in transit

Encryption makes captured traffic difficult to read while it travels through the tunnel.

🏢

Reach private resources

Approved users can connect to internal files, applications or services from outside the office.

🪪

Verify the user

The VPN may require a username, password, certificate and multi-factor authentication.

📍

Create a trusted path

Traffic follows the organisation’s approved VPN gateway and policy.

Simple analogy: The internet is a public road. A VPN tunnel is like a protected vehicle carrying your data between you and an approved destination.

Protection and limits

What a VPN does—and does not do

✓ A VPN can

  • Encrypt traffic through the VPN tunnel
  • Provide remote access to approved company systems
  • Require user and device authentication
  • Apply company network policies
  • Show a VPN gateway address to destinations

✕ A VPN does not

  • Make every website safe
  • Stop all malware or phishing
  • Make a user completely anonymous
  • Replace strong passwords and MFA
  • Protect traffic after it leaves the VPN endpoint
